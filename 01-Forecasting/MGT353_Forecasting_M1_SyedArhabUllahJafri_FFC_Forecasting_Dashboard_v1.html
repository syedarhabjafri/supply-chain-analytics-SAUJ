    FWhat drives demand?

    Season and farm economics move demand most; the price effect cannot be pinned down

    Multiple regression on 144 training weeks (R² 0.60, adjusted 0.58). Each bar is the change in weekly demand associated with the stated change, holding the others constant; whiskers show the 95% confidence interval. These are associations, not proven causes.

    The price coefficient is unstable: per PKR 100 it is +0.05 KT using pre-merger weeks only, −0.6 KT here, −0.9 KT with month dummies and −2.2 KT post-merger, so no elasticity is claimed. Residuals are autocorrelated (Durbin–Watson 1.35); Newey–West errors keep the other drivers significant. The synthetic data were built with these effects, so the regression checks the method, not FFC's market. A scheme week costs about PKR 1,479 per extra bag at 2026 prices.

    HWhat should management do?

    Commit 622 KT for the next 12 weeks with Port Qasim at 50%, and decide its rate from April on Q1 demand

    Commit now

    • Next 12 weeks (29 Dec–22 Mar): produce 622 KT against central demand of 591 KT (range 508–674). Stock stays between 126 and 181 KT.
    • Run Goth Machhi and Mirpur Mathelo at full rate; Port Qasim at 50% (about 7% of capacity held back).
    • Take the turnaround from 23 Feb to 12 Apr: about PKR 3.3 bn less working capital than Oct–Nov in every scenario.

    Keep flexible

    • Port Qasim's rate from April: 100% or 0% (±7% of capacity).
    • Stock target of about 200 KT by 1 Nov, positioned at Sona Stores before 15 Nov.
    • No discount scheme in Q1; schemes only to clear stock above 250 KT.

    Review triggers

    • End-Q1: Q1 demand above 676 KT, Port Qasim to 100%; below 581 KT, Port Qasim off and a July maintenance window.
    • Before 1 Jan: opening stock below 100 KT, run Port Qasim at 100%; above 250 KT, stop it.
    • Output below 58 KT a week in Jan–Feb: keep Port Qasim at 100%.
    • Tracking signal outside ±4 for two weeks, a 4-week miss above 20%, or wheat procurement news: reforecast.

    Key risk

    • A −20% slump still leaves about 306 KT of excess (PKR 9.0 bn).
    • Demand 10% above central loses about 85 KT; capacity at 2025's actual pace loses about 129 KT.
    • Opening stock (assumed 150 KT) and capacity are unverified; both swing the result.
